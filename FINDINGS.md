# Findings (v0, 10 rows)

- **Cloud/API overspend from runaway agent loops is the single most common documented category (4 of 10 rows: INC-004, INC-005, INC-006, INC-008)**, ranging from a $32 personal bill to a $1.8M internal Amazon project, and the common mechanism in every case is the same: no hard per-agent spending cap, so a loop or misjudged task scope burns money until a human notices the invoice (INC-004, INC-005, INC-006, INC-008).
- **The most common "control that would have helped" in the dataset is `per-agent budget`, covering 3 of 10 rows (INC-004, INC-005, INC-006)**, followed by `human approval` on 3 rows (INC-001, INC-003, INC-009), then single occurrences of `payee allowlist` (INC-002), `per-payment cap` (INC-007), `rate/velocity limit` (INC-008) and `credential never exposed to agent` (INC-010).
- **Confirmed real-money manipulated-payment incidents in the wild both involve crypto, not fiat (INC-001 Freysa, $47,316; INC-002 Grok/Bankr, ~$150k-$200k)** -- we found no publicly reported, sourced case of an AI agent completing an unauthorized real-world *fiat* (card/bank) payment outside of a security researcher's test; the closest fiat example is a Guardio Labs research demo against Perplexity Comet (INC-003), not an unprompted victim.
- **Two of the three research-demo rows target protocol/marketplace-level weaknesses rather than one specific product bug**: INC-009 shows Google's AP2 payment-mandate protocol can be steered pre-signature by prompt injection without breaking its cryptographic enforcement, and INC-010 shows a live agent-skills marketplace (ClawHub) hosting a published skill that routes raw card numbers through an LLM's context window by design -- both suggest the exposure is structural (how payment data flows through an agent's reasoning layer), not a one-off coding mistake.
- **Several viral "AI agent ran up a $47,000/$78,000 bill" stories circulating on dev.to and vendor blogs could not be verified as real incidents and were excluded** (see SEARCH-LOG.md) -- for example, the GitHub `vectara/awesome-agent-failures` "LangChain $47k infinite loop" case study checks out as an illustrative teaching example, not a documented real event, which means public discussion of "agent overspend" risk is currently running ahead of the verifiable incident count; INC-005 (DN42, $6,531) and INC-006 (AgentBudget, $32) are the two overspend rows in this log we could trace to a genuine first-hand account.
